    [letter: I] I dreste me forth, and happede to mete anone [leaf]
    Right a faire lady, I you ensure; [leaf]
    And she come riding by herselfe alone, [leaf]
    All in white; with semblance ful demure [leaf]
    I salued her, and bad her good aventure [leaf]
    Might her befall, as I coude most humbly; [leaf]
    And she answered, ‘My doughter, gramercy!’

    [letter: M] ‘Madame,’ quod I, ‘if that I durst enquere [leaf]
    Of you, I woulde faine, of that company, [leaf]
    Wite what they be that paste by this arbere?’ [leaf]
    And she ayen answerede right friendly: [leaf]
    ‘My faire doughter, all tho that passed here by [leaf]
    In white clothing, be servaunts everichone [leaf]
    Unto the Leafe, and I myselfe am one.

    [letter: S] See ye not her that crowned is,’ quod she, [leaf]
    ‘All in white?’—‘Madame,’ quod I, ‘yis:’ [leaf]
    ‘That is Diané, goddesse of chastité; [leaf]
    And for because that she a maiden is, [leaf]
    In her own hond the braunch she beareth iwis, [leaf]
    That agnus castus men calle properly; [leaf]
    And all the ladies in her company,

    [letter: W] Which as ye se of that hearb chapelets weare, [leaf]
    Be such as han kept alway hir maidenheed: [leaf]
    And alle they that of laurer chapelets beare, [leaf]
    Be such as hardy were, and manly indeed, [leaf]
    Victorious name which never may be dede! [leaf]
    And alle they were so worthy of here honde, [leaf]
    That in her time none might hem withstonde.

    [letter: A] And tho that weare chaplets on here hede [leaf]
    Of fresh woodbind, be such as never were [leaf]
    To love untrue in word, in thought, ne dede, [leaf]
    But aye stedfast; ne for pleasaunce, ne fere, [leaf]
    Thogh that they shuld here hertes al to-tere, [leaf]
    Woulde ne flitte, but ever were stedfaste, [leaf]
    Til that here lives there asunder braste.’

    [letter: N] ‘Now faire madame,’ quod I, ‘yet would I pray [leaf]
    Your ladiship, if that it mighte be, [leaf]
    That I mighte knowe, by some maner way, [leaf]
    (Sith that it hath i-liked your beauté, [leaf]
    The trouth of these ladies for to telle me); [leaf]
    What that these knightes be in rich armour, [leaf]
    And what tho be in grene and weare the flour?

    [letter: A] And why that some dide reverence to the tre, [leaf]
    And some unto the plot of floures faire?’ [leaf]
    ‘With right good will, my fair doghter,’ quod she, [leaf]
    ‘Sith your desire is good and debonaire; [leaf]
    Tho nine crowned be very exemplaire [leaf]
    Of all honour longing to chivalry; [leaf]
    And those certaine be called the Nine Worthy,

    [letter: W] Which ye may see now riding all before [leaf]
    That in her time dide many a noble dede, [leaf]
    And for here worthinesse full oft have bore [leaf]
    The crowne of laurer leaves on here hede, [leaf]
    As ye may in your olde bookes rede; [leaf]
    And how that he that was a conquerour, [leaf]
    Hadde by laurer alway his most honour.

    [letter: A] And tho that beare bowes in here honde [leaf]
    Of the precious laurer so notable, [leaf]
    Be such as were, I woll ye understonde, [leaf]
    Noble knightes of the rounde table, [leaf]
    And eke the Douseperis honourable, [leaf]
    Which they bearen in signe of victory; [leaf]
    It is witnesse of here deedes mightily.

    [letter: E] Eke there be knightes old of the garter, [leaf]
    That in her time dide right worthily; [leaf]
    And the honour they dide to the laurer, [leaf]
    Is for by it they have here laud wholly, [leaf]
    Here triumph eke, and marshall glory; [leaf]
    Which unto hem is more parfit richesse, [leaf]
    Than any wight imagine can or gesse.

    [letter: F] For one leafe given of that noble tree [leaf]
    To any wight that hath done worthily, [leaf]
    And it be done so as it oughte to be, [leaf]
    Is more honour than anything earthly; [leaf]
    Witnesse of Rome that founder was truly [leaf]
    Of all knighthood and deedes marvelous; [leaf]
    Record I take of Titus Livius.

    [letter: A] And as for her that crowned is in greene, [leaf]
    It is Flora, of these floures goddesse; [leaf]
    And all that here on her awaiting beene, [leaf]
    It are such folk that loved idelnesse, [leaf]
    And not delite hadde of no businesse, [leaf]
    But for to hunt and hauke, and pley in medes, [leaf]
    And many other such idle dedes.

    [letter: A] And for the greate delite and pleasaunce [leaf]
    They have to the floure, and so reverently [leaf]
    They unto it do such grete obeisaunce [leaf]
    As ye may se.’—‘Now faire Madame,’ quod I, [leaf]
    ‘If I durst aske what is the cause and why, [leaf]
    That knightes have the signe of honour, [leaf]
    Wel rather by the leafe than by the flour?’

    [letter: S] ‘Soothly, doughter,’ quod she, ‘this is the trouth: [leaf]
    For knightes ever should be persevering, [leaf]
    To seeke honour without feintise or slouth, [leaf]
    Fro wele to better in all manner thing; [leaf]
    In signe of which, with leaves aye lasting [leaf]
    They be rewarded after here degré, [leaf]
    Whose lusty green may not appaired be,

    [letter: B] But aie keping here beautie fresh & greene;
    For there nis storme that ne may hem deface, [leaf]
    Ne haile nor snow, ne winde nor frostes kene; [leaf]
    Wherfore they have this propertie and grace.
    And for the floure, within a little space [leaf]
    Woll be i-lost, so simple of nature [leaf]
    They be, that they no greevance may endure;

    [letter: A] And every storme will blow hem soone awaye, [leaf]
    Ne laste they not but for oon season;
    That is the cause, the very trouth to saye, [leaf]
    That they maye not, by no way of reason, [leaf]
    Be put to no such occupation.’ [leaf]
    ‘Madame,’ quod I, ‘with all mine whole servise [leaf]
    I thanke you now, in my most humble wise;

    [letter: F] For now I am acertained throughly, [leaf]
    Of every thing I desired to knowe.’ [leaf]
    ‘I am right glad that I have said, sothly, [leaf]
    Ought to your pleasure, if ye wille me trowe,’ [leaf]
    Quod she ayen, ‘but to whom do ye owe [leaf]
    Your service? and which wolle ye honoure, [leaf]
    Tel me I pray, this yere, the Leafe or the Floure?’

    [letter: M] ‘Madame,’ quod I, ‘though I be least worthy, [leaf]
    Unto the Leafe I owe mine observaunce’:
    ‘That is,’ quod she, ‘right well done certainly;
    And pray I God to honour you avaunce, [leaf]
    And kepe you fro the wicked remembraunce [leaf]
    Of Malebouch, and all his crueltie, [leaf]
    And all that good and well conditioned be.

    [letter: F] For here may I no lenger now abide, [leaf]
    I muste followe the greate company, [leaf]
    That ye maye see yonder before you ride.’
    And tho forth, as I couthe, most humbly, [leaf]
    I tooke my leve of her, as she gan hie [leaf]
    After hem as fast as ever she mighte; [leaf]
    And I drow homeward, for it was nigh nighte,

    [letter: A] And put all that I hadde seene in writing, [leaf]
    Under support of hem that lust it to rede. [leaf]
    O little booke, thou art so unconning, [leaf]
    How darst thou put thyself in prees, for drede? [leaf]
    It is wonder that thou wexest not rede! [leaf]
    Sith that thou wost full lite who shall beholde [leaf]
    Thy rude language, ful boistously unfolde.
